Write the Brief Before the Click

Five Fields to Fill Before You Open a Window

A Shopping Brief records your needs before any listing tries to convince you. Fill the five fields below first, then let the brief do the filtering.

Shopping Brief — One Category, One Question
Product typeState exactly what you are looking for — a structured crossbody bag, not “a bag.”
Intended useWrite the real purpose — daily commute, travel, a gift — before the style or price.
Must haveList the conditions that cannot be dropped — size, material, a closing flap, a minimum width.
PreferRecord what would be nice but is not required — a color, a lining, a brand.
Still need to verifyKeep the questions to re-confirm on the current page — strap length, hardware, fit.

Must / Prefer / Ignore

Sort Every Attribute Into One Tab

A low price, a pretty picture, or a popular label cannot prove a candidate fits. Sort each signal into one of three tabs so it cannot masquerade as a need.

Non-negotiable conditions

Must is a hard requirement. If the candidate fails a must-have, it is out — no discount, image, or label changes that. Keep this list short and concrete.

Nice-to-have, not required

Prefer is a bonus that can tilt a choice between two candidates that both pass every must-have. It never overrides a missing must-have.

Eye-catching, not evidence

Ignore covers the markers that grab attention but prove nothing — low price, glossy photo, hot label. They stay outside the brief.

One Aisle, One Question

Shrink a Broad Interest to a Single Aisle

A vague interest produces an endless aisle. Narrow it to one category and one question that a listing can actually answer.

  1. Name the broad interest. Start from the fuzzy want — “something for a trip.”

  2. Choose one category. Pick a single window from the twelve above instead of browsing every aisle at once.

  3. Reduce it to one question. Turn the interest into a yes-or-no question a listing can answer — “does it fit a 13″ laptop?”

  4. Handle one question at a time. Answer it, record it in the brief, then move to the next. Do not run all questions in parallel.

Walk one doorway at a time. A broad interest is a storefront with many doors. A brief walks through one aisle, with one question, and does not double back until it is answered.

The Storefront Test

Hold a Candidate Against the Brief

Before trusting a window display, hold the candidate up to the brief and check what passes a must-have and what is only a bright marker.

✓Passes the brief

  • Matches the stated product type.
  • Serves the written intended use.
  • Meets every must-have condition.
  • Preferences only break ties.

✕Only bright in the window

  • A different product type than written.
  • Fails one or more must-haves.
  • A low price or glossy photo doing the talking.
  • A hot label standing in for a real need.
Storefront ruleA candidate that fails a must-have is out regardless of how good it looks in the window. Only the brief decides what belongs.

Know When to Leave

Set Your Stop Rules in Advance

  • 1

    No product type is written. Without a type, every aisle looks relevant. Stop and write the type first.

  • 2

    The category keeps drifting. Two or more categories at once means no single question. Stop and pick one window.

  • 3

    A must-have is missing. A candidate fails a hard condition but looks attractive. Stop and set it aside, do not soften the rule.

  • 4

    Open questions are growing. More questions than answers means you are collecting, not deciding. Stop and re-check on the current page.

  • 5

    The brief has not changed the outcome. When the brief no longer filters anything, pause and rewrite it before continuing.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an EastMallBuy spreadsheet?

An EastMallBuy spreadsheet is a shared document that compiles listing references, category groupings, and product links formatted for use with agent shopping services. It is a reference to research, not an official, live, or complete product catalog.

Is this website the official EastMallBuy service?

No. This website is an independent informational and navigation resource. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or operated by EastMallBuy or Google.

What is a Shopping Brief?

A Shopping Brief is a short written plan you make before browsing. It records the product type, the intended use, the must-have conditions, the nice-to-have preferences, and the questions you still need to verify, so eye-catching markers do not replace your real needs.

Does this site rank products or recommend purchases?

No. This resource does not rank products, assign scores, or name automatic winners. It is not a store, a link converter, a QC database, a price tracker, a shipping calculator, or an order tracking tool.

How do I write a Shopping Brief before browsing?

Write down the product type first, then the intended use, then your must-have conditions, then your nice-to-have preferences, and finally the questions you still need to verify. A low price, a pretty picture, or a popular label cannot prove a candidate fits those needs.

Where do I continue after writing my brief?

After you have written your brief and re-checked the destination page, you can continue through an agent shopping service to submit the item. This website is a navigation resource and does not process orders itself.
