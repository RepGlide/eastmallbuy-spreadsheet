Category Scope Guide

Narrow a broad interest to one category and one question a listing can actually answer.

Scope starts with a single aisle

A broad interest — “something nice for a trip” — is a storefront with many doors. Without a scope, every aisle looks relevant and the search never ends. Narrowing to one category and one question turns an endless corridor into a single walkable aisle.

Step 1 — Name the broad interest

Start from the fuzzy want. Say it plainly: “something for a trip,” “an upgrade for work,” “a gift.” The interest is the starting point, not the plan. Naming it keeps the later narrowing honest.

Step 2 — Choose one category

Pick a single window from the twelve category windows on the home page. Bags, sneakers, watches, or another group — but only one. Trying to hold two categories at once means no single question can guide you.

Step 3 — Reduce it to one question

Turn the interest into a yes-or-no question a listing can actually answer. “Does it fit a 13″ laptop?” is answerable. “Is it good?” is not. An answerable question is what moves a search forward.

Step 4 — Handle one question at a time

Answer the question, record the answer in the brief, then move to the next one. Running many questions in parallel produces a pile of half-answers, not a decision. One aisle, one question, one answer.

Scope ruleA category is scoped when it has one name and one answerable question. Two categories or an unanswerable question means the scope is still open.
✓Scoped: Bags → “does it fit a 13″ laptop?”
?Open: “something for a trip” → still spread across four windows.

A worked narrowing

  1. Broad interest: “something for a trip.”
  2. One category: Bags.
  3. One question: “does it fit a 13″ laptop and close with a flap?”
  4. Answer: re-checked on the current page, then recorded in the brief.
